RACI Matrix

Seven recurring decisions across the Crawl, Walk and Run phases

  • A Accountable: owns the outcome, exactly one per decision
  • R Responsible: does the work
  • C Consulted: input before the decision
  • I Informed: notified after, no input rights
  •    Teal rows = roadmap-specific decisions
Seven recurring transformation decisions with their Accountable, Responsible, Consulted and Informed roles, and the rationale for each Accountable role. Rows 6 and 7 are roadmap-specific decisions.
# Recurring decision Accountable Responsible Consulted Informed Why this role is Accountable
1 Budget allocation: how transformation funding is split across InControl and Factory of the Future initiatives each phase EVP & CFO VP, Transformation Office; segment finance leads CEO; EVP & COO; SVP & CIO; Segment EVPs Board Audit Committee; Regional Ops Leaders The CFO owns capital allocation and the forecast investors judge Jabil on, so transformation spend must reconcile to those numbers. The roadmap ties Run funding to proven Walk results (43% supply chain cost cut, 80% defect prediction), and the CFO is the neutral party who can hold every initiative to that bar.
2 Architecture standards: approving or overriding technology choices that affect the shared platform SVP & CIO Enterprise Architecture lead; CISO EVP & COO; Segment EVPs; VP, Enterprise Data & Analytics EVP & CFO; Site Directors Initiatives 4 and 6 only scale if every factory feeds the same Azure foundation, so one role must be able to block a site's or segment's preferred tool. The CIO owns that platform and carries the cost when integrations break.
3 Data governance: data quality standards and access policy across business units SVP & CIO VP, Enterprise Data & Analytics; BU data stewards EVP & General Counsel; CISO; Segment EVPs; SVP, Global Procurement CHRO; Site Directors InControl's value is normalizing fragmented regional data into one pipeline, which fails if each business unit sets its own definitions. The CIO controls the systems where quality rules and customer-data segregation are enforced, and no single segment EVP can own a policy spanning all three segments.
4 Initiative prioritization: what gets built next when people and budget are constrained EVP & COO VP, Transformation Office; Enterprise Architecture lead Segment EVPs; SVP & CIO; EVP & CFO; SVP, Global Procurement CEO; CHRO; Site Directors Most of the roadmap lands on factory floors and procurement desks the COO runs, so the COO sees when two rollouts would hit the same site in the same quarter. Prioritizing inside the CFO's funding envelopes keeps this from becoming a second budget decision.
5 Vendor and partnership decisions: approving and governing key external relationships SVP, Global Procurement & Supply Chain SVP & CIO (technical evaluation); Kymeta account team EVP & General Counsel; CISO; EVP & CFO Board (above approval threshold); Site Directors Procurement owns contract terms, supplier risk and spend leverage across Jabil's footprint, and Microsoft becomes a long-term dependency once Initiative 6 scales. The CIO judges technical fit, but commercial commitment and partner performance need one owner who sees every relationship.

RACI check and governance boundaries

RACI check: one Accountable per row; all five required decision types (rows 1-5) plus two roadmap-specific decisions (rows 6-7); no role is both A and R on a row.

Boundaries: the CFO sets each initiative's funding envelope (row 1) and the COO sequences work inside it (row 4); the CEO approves scaling (row 6) and the COO governs how AI is adopted once scaled (row 7).

Titles: CEO, CFO, COO, General Counsel and EVPs, Global Business Units are disclosed Jabil roles; Procurement, Data, Quality and Transformation Office titles are realistic assumptions.
